Introduction to Temporal Dependence

  • Cross Section Time Series

  • Spatial is built on time series.

  • We assume independent observations – in time series this is not true.

    • unemployment this month is related to last month

    • snow in the rockies this season is related to last season.

  • Non-independence will screw up our assumptions of OLS

  • we want our errors normally distributed

    • no heteroskedasticity

    • no autocorrelation

    • however in time series, often our OLS assumptions are not believable.

      • error is where we will see this.

        • im going to see weird things in error – is there something wrong with my model? or is there a correction?

          • any time we fail to correctly specify a dynamic process, those omitted variables end up in the error term.

  • Time series is nice because we can usually just use OLS

  • t subscripts instead of i.

  • panel data will be t and i

  • errors may not be independent!

  • Errors may not have constant variance over time! Violate assumption of no heteroskedasticity.

    • error variance is changing over time.

Characteristics of Time Series Data

  • Data are sampled consistently

  • order matters; a time variable is required in a time series dataset.

  • Time series is a data generating process.

  • missing data are problematic

Lags

  • order matters

  • data that are more proximate to one another tend to be more correlated. Time version of Toeblers law.

  • we account for this in our model by using lags: \(y_{t-1}, y_{t-2},..\)

  • lags will become extremely useful in our models as we will use them to condition for the effect…

  • you lose an observation when you take a lag.

Difference

  • the difference of a series is another common transformation

  • the first-diff measures the change between t-1 and t of a series. This fundamentally changes our data and possibly underlying relationships.

  • double diff is possible but rare

Steps:

  • Plot!

  • Diagnose Stationarity

    • sationarity is very important! Sationarity is when the characteristics are:

      • mean reverting ~ stable mean

      • constant variance

      • constant covariance

    • i need my data to be stationary!

      • if you dont, you cant believe the inferences you are showing me.

    • Autoregression

      • lags contain past information within in it.

        • it depends on your question but y_t-1 will contains information from older time periods within it.

    • Positive \(\Phi\) current values of \(y_t\) are positively related to past ones.

    • as \(\Phi\) -> 1 the relationship across periods is stronger.

    • If \(\Phi\) = 0 there is no autocorrelation.

Unit Root

  • Series with constant mean, variance, and convariance are said to be stationary

  • A unit root means you have violated one of the assumptions of stationarity

    • usually the variance stationarity.

    • instead of I(0) it is I(1)

  • if unit root exists just take first difference.

  • hard part is testing for unit roots.

How to Test for Unit Roots?

  • Dickey-Fuller unit root test is the older and still most popular

  • you subtract \(y_{t-1}\) for both sides

  • null hypothesis series contains a unit root

    • if we reject null then series is stationary

Introduction to Spatial Modeling

  • spatial analysis is similar to time.

  • space is more difficult to deal with because space is in multiple directions

  • space as an econometric problem.

  • space is not just geographic

  • space is not a nuissance

  • space = autocorrelation

  • system versus actor centered

  • common exposure is not spatial

  • contagion is.

  • space is substance

    • dont account then ovb

  • Galton’s problem: refers to the importance of determining whether an outcome was caused via an external or internal factor.

  • need to identify spatial autocorrelaiton

What causes spatial association

  • autocorelation and association is not the same.

  • Common exposure as a cause of spatial association:

    • when individuals in a subset of the population are exposed commonly to a stimulus

  • Spatial interdependence is DIFFERENT!

  • contagion and common exposure are different BUT they can be intterrelated.

  • contagion is a network process basically

Spatial Error Model

  • classic assumption – errors are iid

    • the iid is very important – nothing systematic in the errors

      • iid implies randomness

    • longitudinal data also has this problem

  • allows errors to be correlated

  • we are modelling the error that is iid

    • and then modelling the error that is spatial in nature.

    • w is a weight

      • weighting is theoretical. Closeness may be ideological or geographical

        • W_i is like a distance measure.

  • W is the connectivity matrix and links outcomes in j to i.

  • spatial error model not used really in political science

    • what does it mean to say that space matters in error but not in substance?
